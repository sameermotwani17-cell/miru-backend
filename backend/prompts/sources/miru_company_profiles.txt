MIRU - Detailed Company Profiles for AI Interview Simulation

This document provides the detailed company profiles required for the
MIRU AI interview agent. Each profile is structured to align with the
MIRU Sid Research Brief, focusing on the cultural nuances, HR personas,
and specific hiring criteria needed to simulate a realistic and
culturally-aware Japanese job interview.

1. Rakuten Group, Inc.

ONE-LINE HIRING PERSONALITY: Aggressively global, data-driven, and
meritocratic, seeking entrepreneurial-minded individuals who thrive on
speed and measurable results.

OFFICIAL HIRING CRITERIA (from their career page --- direct quote
preferred): Rakuten Shugi, which comprises Brand Concepts and the
Five Principles for Success, is the set of core principles that all
employees of the Rakuten Group follow. We are looking for people who
understand and can execute Rakuten Shugi.

REAL INTERVIEW FOCUS (from Glassdoor and expat reports --- what
actually happens): Interviews heavily test for alignment with the
Five Principles for Success. Candidates are often asked to provide
specific examples from their past that demonstrate these principles. The
Englishnization policy is real; interviews are often conducted
partially or fully in English, even for Japanese candidates. There's a
strong emphasis on data and KPI-driven thinking. Expect questions about
how you would measure success in a given role.

CEO / FOUNDER PHILOSOPHY (that bleeds into hiring): Hiroshi
Mikitani's philosophy, often summarized as Get Things Done, is
central to Rakuten's culture. His book, Marketplace 3.0, is a major
influence. The core idea is that success comes from a combination of
hypothesis, practice, validation, and systematization (Shikumika).
This scientific approach to business is a key part of the company's DNA
and is reflected in the interview process.

3 COMPANY-SPECIFIC INTERVIEW QUESTIONS:

-   Q1: Describe a time you had to achieve a difficult goal with a
    tight deadline. How did you apply the principle of 'Speed!! Speed!!
    Speed!!' to succeed?

    -   Why they ask it: To assess a candidate's ability to work
        under pressure and their understanding of Rakuten's emphasis on
        rapid execution.

-   Q2: Imagine you are launching a new service. How would you use
    the 'Hypothesize -> Practice -> Validate -> Shikumika' cycle to
    ensure its success?

    -   Why they ask it: To test for a data-driven and analytical
        mindset, and to see if the candidate understands Rakuten's core
        problem-solving methodology.

-   Q3: Rakuten's mission is to empower people and society. How
    does your personal career goal align with this mission?

    -   Why they ask it: To gauge a candidate's long-term
        commitment and cultural fit, and to see if they are motivated by
        more than just salary.

THE ONE THING THAT GETS YOU REJECTED HERE (specific to this
company): Aversion to data and metrics. If a candidate cannot or will
not quantify their achievements and speak in terms of KPIs, they will be
seen as a poor fit for Rakuten's highly analytical culture.

FOREIGNER NOTES (anything specific about how this company treats
foreign applicants): Rakuten is one of the most foreigner-friendly
large companies in Japan. The Englishnization policy makes it an
attractive option for non-Japanese speakers. However, the company still
expects a high level of professionalism and a deep understanding of its
corporate philosophy. Foreigners are not exempt from knowing and
embodying Rakuten Shugi.

2. Toyota Motor Corporation

ONE-LINE HIRING PERSONALITY: Traditional, methodical, and deeply
committed to long-term improvement, seeking humble team players who
respect the process.

OFFICIAL HIRING CRITERIA (from their career page --- direct quote
preferred): The Toyota Way is supported by two main pillars:
'Continuous Improvement' and 'Respect for People'. We are never
satisfied with where we are and always work to improve our business by
putting forward new ideas and working to the best of our abilities. We
respect all Toyota stakeholders, and believe the success of our business
is created by individual effort and good teamwork.

REAL INTERVIEW FOCUS (from Glassdoor and expat reports --- what
actually happens): Interviews are highly structured and focus on a
candidate's thought process. The concept of Genchi Genbutsu (go and
see for yourself) is frequently tested through hypothetical
problem-solving scenarios. Interviewers are less interested in flashy
achievements and more interested in a candidate's ability to identify
root causes and propose incremental, sustainable improvements. Loyalty
and a desire for long-term employment are heavily screened for.

CEO / FOUNDER PHILOSOPHY (that bleeds into hiring): The principles
of the Toyota Production System (TPS), developed by Taiichi Ohno and
Eiji Toyoda, are the foundation of the company's culture. The Toyota
Way 2001 is a formalization of these principles. The core idea is the
complete elimination of waste and a deep respect for people. This
philosophy is so ingrained that it's not just a business strategy;
it's a way of life at Toyota.

3 COMPANY-SPECIFIC INTERVIEW QUESTIONS:

-   Q1: Imagine you are a line manager and your team is
    consistently failing to meet its production targets. How would you
    apply the principle of 'Genchi Genbutsu' to identify and solve the
    problem?

    -   Why they ask it: To assess a candidate's problem-solving
        methodology and their understanding of Toyota's hands-on,
        evidence-based approach.

-   Q2: Describe a time you made a mistake at work. What was the
    mistake, and what steps did you take to ensure it would not happen
    again?

    -   Why they ask it: To evaluate a candidate's commitment to
        continuous improvement (Kaizen) and their willingness to take
        responsibility for their actions.

-   Q3: Why do you want to work for Toyota, and where do you see
    yourself in our company in 10 years?

    -   Why they ask it: To screen for long-term commitment
        (Chuuseishin) and to see if the candidate's career goals align
        with Toyota's focus on internal development and lifetime
        employment.

THE ONE THING THAT GETS YOU REJECTED HERE (specific to this
company): Appearing to be a job-hopper or expressing a desire for
rapid, individual promotion. Toyota values loyalty and team-oriented
career growth above all else.

FOREIGNER NOTES (anything specific about how this company treats
foreign applicants): Toyota is a traditional Japanese company, and
while it has a global presence, its headquarters in Japan can be
challenging for foreigners to integrate into. A high level of Japanese
language proficiency is often required, and a deep respect for Japanese
business etiquette is essential. Foreigners who demonstrate a genuine
interest in and understanding of the Toyota Way will have a significant
advantage.

3. Sony Group Corporation

ONE-LINE HIRING PERSONALITY: A unique blend of creative artistry and
disciplined engineering, seeking innovators who can dream and execute
with precision.

OFFICIAL HIRING CRITERIA (from their career page --- direct quote
preferred): Fill the world with emotion, through the power of
creativity and technology. To be a part of Sony is to be a part of a
collective of people who are relentlessly curious and creative, and who
know that we can make a difference in the world by bringing our ideas to
life.

REAL INTERVIEW FOCUS (from Glassdoor and expat reports --- what
actually happens): Interviews at Sony often explore a candidate's
passion for the company's products and services. Expect questions about
your favorite Sony product and how you would improve it. There is a
strong emphasis on both creativity and technical knowledge. Candidates
are often asked to demonstrate their ability to think outside the box
while also showing a deep understanding of their field. The interview
process can be quite formal and traditional, despite the company's
creative image.

CEO / FOUNDER PHILOSOPHY (that bleeds into hiring): Sony's culture
is heavily influenced by its founders, Masaru Ibuka and Akio Morita.
Their philosophy of doing what has never been done before is still a
core part of the company's identity. The current leadership continues
to emphasize the importance of Kando (emotional involvement) and the
fusion of creativity and technology. This means they are looking for
people who are not just skilled, but also passionate about creating new
and exciting experiences.

3 COMPANY-SPECIFIC INTERVIEW QUESTIONS:

-   Q1: What is your favorite Sony product, and what is one
    improvement you would make to it?

    -   Why they ask it: To assess a candidate's passion for
        Sony's products and their ability to think critically and
        creatively about product development.

-   Q2: Describe a project where you had to combine your creative
    and technical skills to solve a problem.

    -   Why they ask it: To evaluate a candidate's ability to
        bridge the gap between creativity and technology, a core value
        at Sony.

-   Q3: Sony's purpose is to 'fill the world with emotion.' How
    can you contribute to this purpose in the role you are applying
    for?

    -   Why they ask it: To gauge a candidate's understanding of
        Sony's corporate philosophy and their ability to connect their
        personal goals with the company's mission.

THE ONE THING THAT GETS YOU REJECTED HERE (specific to this
company): Lack of passion for Sony's products and mission. If a
candidate seems indifferent to the company's work or unable to
articulate why they want to work for Sony specifically, they will be
seen as a poor cultural fit.

FOREIGNER NOTES (anything specific about how this company treats
foreign applicants): Sony has a global presence and is generally open
to hiring foreigners, especially those with specialized technical
skills. However, for positions at the Tokyo headquarters, a high level
of Japanese language proficiency is often required. Foreigners who can
demonstrate a deep appreciation for Japanese culture and Sony's unique
history will have an advantage.

4. SoftBank Group Corp.

ONE-LINE HIRING PERSONALITY: A highly aggressive and ambitious
investment group, looking for visionary thinkers who are comfortable
with risk and driven by a desire to be number one.

OFFICIAL HIRING CRITERIA (from their career page --- direct quote
preferred): The SoftBank Group is a corporate group that will
continue to grow for 300 years. We are looking for people who share our
vision and are willing to take on challenges.

REAL INTERVIEW FOCUS (from Glassdoor and expat reports --- what
actually happens): Interviews are fast-paced and challenging, with a
strong focus on a candidate's ambition and ability to think
strategically. Expect to be asked about your long-term goals and how
they align with SoftBank's 300-year vision. The concept of Reverse
Planning is often tested, where candidates are asked to start with a
bold goal and work backward to create a plan. There is a heavy emphasis
on financial and analytical skills, even for non-finance roles.

CEO / FOUNDER PHILOSOPHY (that bleeds into hiring): Masayoshi Son's
audacious and often controversial leadership style is the driving force
behind SoftBank's culture. His philosophy is centered on the
Information Revolution and the idea that technology can solve
humanity's greatest challenges. He is known for his long-term vision
and his willingness to make huge bets on unproven technologies. This
philosophy attracts a certain type of employee: one who is not afraid of
failure and is motivated by the opportunity to change the world.

3 COMPANY-SPECIFIC INTERVIEW QUESTIONS:

-   Q1: What is your personal 30-year plan? How does it align with
    SoftBank's 300-year vision?

    -   Why they ask it: To assess a candidate's long-term thinking
        and ambition, and to see if they are a good fit for SoftBank's
        visionary culture.

-   Q2: If you were given a $100 million investment, what
    technology or company would you invest in and why?

    -   Why they ask it: To evaluate a candidate's strategic
        thinking, risk tolerance, and understanding of emerging
        technologies.

-   Q3: Describe a time you failed. What did you learn from the
    experience, and how did it make you stronger?

    -   Why they ask it: To gauge a candidate's resilience and
        their ability to learn from their mistakes, a key trait for
        success in SoftBank's high-stakes environment.

THE ONE THING THAT GETS YOU REJECTED HERE (specific to this
company): Thinking too small. SoftBank is looking for people who are
not afraid to dream big and take on massive challenges. Candidates who
are too conservative or risk-averse will not be a good fit for the
company's culture.

FOREIGNER NOTES (anything specific about how this company treats
foreign applicants): SoftBank is a global company with a diverse
workforce. However, the company's leadership is predominantly Japanese,
and a deep understanding of Japanese business culture is still
important. Foreigners who can demonstrate a strong work ethic, a
willingness to learn, and a passion for technology will be
well-received.

5. Fast Retailing Co., Ltd. (Uniqlo)

ONE-LINE HIRING PERSONALITY: Customer-centric and globally
ambitious, seeking adaptable individuals who embody the LifeWear
philosophy and a strong sense of ownership.

OFFICIAL HIRING CRITERIA (from their career page --- direct quote
preferred): Changing clothes. Changing conventional wisdom. Change
the world. We are looking for people who share our mission to create
truly great clothing with new and unique value, bringing joy and
satisfaction to people worldwide.

REAL INTERVIEW FOCUS (from Glassdoor and expat reports --- what
actually happens): Interviews often focus on a candidate's
understanding of the LifeWear concept and their ability to embody
it. Expect questions about customer service scenarios and how you would
handle difficult situations. There's a strong emphasis on teamwork,
adaptability, and a proactive approach to problem-solving. Candidates
are often assessed on their potential to become future leaders and
managers, even for entry-level positions, reflecting the Zen-in
Keiei (Global One) philosophy.

CEO / FOUNDER PHILOSOPHY (that bleeds into hiring): Tadashi Yanai,
the founder and CEO of Fast Retailing, is known for his aggressive
global expansion strategy and his belief in the power of clothing to
change lives. His philosophy of Zen-in Keiei (Global One) encourages
every employee to think and act like a business owner. He emphasizes
speed, innovation, and a relentless focus on the customer. This
translates into a hiring process that seeks individuals who are not just
employees, but potential entrepreneurs within the company.

3 COMPANY-SPECIFIC INTERVIEW QUESTIONS:

-   Q1: Describe a time you went above and beyond to satisfy a
    customer. What was the situation, and what was the outcome?

    -   Why they ask it: To assess a candidate's customer-centric
        mindset and their commitment to providing exceptional service, a
        core value of Uniqlo.

-   Q2: How do you interpret Uniqlo's 'LifeWear' philosophy,
    and how do you think you can contribute to it in this role?

    -   Why they ask it: To gauge a candidate's understanding of
        the brand's core concept and their ability to align their
        personal values with the company's mission.

-   Q3: If you were a store manager, what is one innovative idea
    you would implement to improve sales or customer experience?

    -   Why they ask it: To evaluate a candidate's entrepreneurial
        spirit, problem-solving skills, and potential for leadership
        within the company.

THE ONE THING THAT GETS YOU REJECTED HERE (specific to this
company): Lack of initiative or a passive attitude. Uniqlo values
employees who are proactive, take ownership of their work, and are
constantly looking for ways to improve, rather than waiting to be told
what to do.

FOREIGNER NOTES (anything specific about how this company treats
foreign applicants): Fast Retailing is actively expanding globally and
seeks diverse talent. While Japanese language proficiency is highly
valued, especially for customer-facing roles in Japan, there are also
opportunities for non-Japanese speakers in various departments. Foreign
applicants who demonstrate a strong understanding of the brand, a
passion for retail, and a willingness to adapt to a fast-paced,
demanding environment will be well-regarded.
